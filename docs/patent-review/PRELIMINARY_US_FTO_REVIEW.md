# fear.social Preliminary U.S. Patent Freedom-to-Operate Review

**Review date:** July 20, 2026  
**Product reviewed:** Current fear.social web application and Cloudflare backend  
**Jurisdiction and scope:** Published and granted U.S. patents most directly associated with Meta/Facebook/Instagram, ByteDance/TikTok, LinkedIn/Microsoft, and exact-name searches for VaynerMedia and Complex Media.  
**Purpose:** Engineering risk triage and product design guidance, not a legal opinion.

## Executive conclusion

The current product is **not an obvious claim-for-claim copy** of the social-platform patents reviewed. Its highest-scrutiny areas are feed ordering, people discovery, opportunity relevance, short-form media, notifications, and social-graph actions. In each area, the current implementation omits one or more material limitations present in the most relevant independent claims.

The strongest current separations are:

- The feed uses a deterministic, human-readable score. It does not train, calibrate, select, or periodically retrain a machine-learning ranking model.
- The product does not collect dwell-time or activation-event telemetry to predict a viewer state.
- People discovery is not generated by comparing corresponding profile parts or calculating profile-similarity subscores.
- Opportunity relevance is lexical overlap with user-supplied profile fields. It does not parse resumes, predict job offers, infer applicant skills from an applicant corpus, or generate advice for increasing a predicted hiring score.
- Posts are persistent. There is no Stories-style ephemeral collection, no ephemeral ranking model, and no direct-message probability used to rank media.
- Comments and notifications are chronological, not model-ranked.
- There is no TikTok-style one-item vertical swipe playback interface or friend-to-friend video recommendation control embedded in comments or forwarding.

This is a **preliminary low-to-moderate residual risk assessment**, not a finding of non-infringement. Patent infringement analysis compares every limitation of an asserted claim with the accused product, and legal status, claim construction, continuations, ownership, prosecution history, and doctrine-of-equivalents questions require U.S. patent counsel. The USPTO likewise explains that infringement primarily involves comparing claim language to the accused product or process.

## Method

1. Inventoried the implemented behavior in `src/App.jsx`, `functions/api/[[path]].js`, and the database migrations.
2. Searched by assignee, product concept, and patent family for feed ranking, recommendations, social graphs, profiles, jobs, video, ephemeral media, notifications, comments, and graphical interfaces.
3. Read the independent claim language rather than relying on titles or abstracts.
4. Compared required claim elements with the present architecture.
5. Treated legal status shown by Google Patents as a research signal only. Counsel must confirm ownership, terminal disclaimers, maintenance fees, expiration, continuations, reexaminations, and enforceability in USPTO Patent Center immediately before relying on status.

The governing statute includes direct, induced, and contributory infringement. See [35 U.S.C. § 271](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A35+section%3A271+edition%3Aprelim%29). The [USPTO patent management guide](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/manage) explains that claim language is central to infringement comparison and that utility-patent enforceability can depend on term and maintenance.

## Current implementation map

| Product surface | Current implementation | Deliberate separation |
|---|---|---|
| Your Path feed | Client-side deterministic score using explicit profile terms, follows, post type, visible engagement counts, media presence, and recency | No ML, embeddings, dwell time, affinity aggregate, calibration, user cohort, or retraining |
| Following feed | Chronological filtering of directly followed authors; official fear.social posts excluded | No recommendation model or second-pass ranker |
| New people | First available unblocked accounts from backend order | No reference-profile comparison, part-by-part subscores, graph-path score, or inferred similarity |

| Comments | Chronological | No comment ranking |

| DMs | User-selected conversations | No suggested recipient based on message text, social path, or inferred relationship |

## Claim-oriented review

### Meta, Facebook, and Instagram

#### US 7,827,208 B2: personalized feed stories

Source: [US7827208B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7827208)

The first independent claim is directed to storing member actions and relationship data, identifying common elements, aggregating actions around those elements, and generating a story describing an action, a user, and other users who acted on the common element. fear.social displays user-authored posts and does not synthesize aggregated social-activity stories around a shared object.

**Preliminary risk:** Low for the current feed.  
**Status note:** Google Patents displayed the patent as active with an expected 2026 expiration. Confirm the exact term and enforceability in Patent Center.

#### US 8,768,863 B2: adaptive news-feed ranking

Source: [US8768863B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8768863B2/en)

The reviewed independent claim requires sets of users sharing attributes, a ranking model trained for each set using interaction features, ranking with that model, and periodic retraining. fear.social has a single readable scoring function and no training or retraining pipeline.

**Preliminary risk:** Low under the present architecture.  
**Re-review trigger:** Any learned model, cohort-specific model, automatic weight fitting, or scheduled retraining.

#### US 9,582,786 B2: ranking model based on viewer social information

Source: [US9582786B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9582786B2/en)

The independent claim requires interaction information, aggregate measures from connected users weighted by affinity, training a first model, training a viewer-interaction model, and choosing between models based on an interaction threshold. The current score contains no learned affinity, connected-user aggregate, dual model, or threshold-based model selection.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.

#### US 10,990,635 B2: ranking ephemeral content

Source: [US10990635B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10990635B2/en)

The claim requires ephemeral content collections and machine-learned probabilities that the viewer will select a collection and send a direct message to its author. fear.social has persistent posts and uses neither probability.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Re-review trigger:** Stories, expiring media collections, or ranking based on predicted DMs.

#### US 11,405,348 B2: managing ephemeral posts

Source: [US11405348B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11405348B2/en)

The family concerns lifecycle and presentation behavior for ephemeral posts. fear.social does not expire posts or implement Stories.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Re-review trigger:** Any automatic expiration, story tray, or timed post lifecycle.

#### US 9,152,675 B2: ranking comments

Source: [US9152675B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9152675B2/en)

fear.social returns comments chronologically and does not calculate or display ranked comment order.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Re-review trigger:** “Top comments,” quality scores, relationship-weighted comments, or learned comment ordering.

#### US 10,643,148 B2: mobile-local-signal feed ranking

Source: [US10643148B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10643148B2/en)

The reviewed family uses local mobile signals in content ranking. fear.social does not use network speed, sensor state, local hardware conditions, or device-state signals to order posts.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.

#### Additional Meta families screened

- [US20190155914A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190155914A1/en), ranking based on predicted original-broadcast behavior: no posting-propensity prediction in fear.social.
- [US11405348B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11405348B2/en), ephemeral lifecycle: no ephemeral posts.
- [US9177291B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9177291B2/en), chained connection requests: fear.social follows are direct and do not propagate a chain request.
- [US10122672B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10122672B2/en), group-content notifications based on interaction: fear.social does not interaction-rank group notifications.

### LinkedIn and Microsoft

#### US 10,552,428 B2: first-pass ranker calibration

Source: [US10552428B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10552428B2)

The independent claim requires updates from two source categories, separate preliminary ranker models producing raw scores, statistical calibration, and a second-pass ranker. fear.social has no model categories, calibration model, or two-pass learned ranker.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.

#### US 9,811,569 B2 family: similar candidate profiles

Source: [US9811569B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9811569B2/en)

The independent claim requires a reference profile, comparison of corresponding profile parts, part-specific subscores, and selection based on the resulting scores or an extremum. The current “New people” list is not personalized and does not compute profile similarity.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Product safeguard applied:** Renamed “Suggested people” to “New people” so the interface accurately reflects the absence of a similarity-ranking system.

#### US 9,305,287 B2: career-requirement score and suggestion

Source: [US9305287B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9305287B2/en)

The claim requires a data object that includes a resume and an employment requirement, a score representing likelihood of receiving a job offer, and a suggestion for increasing that score. Deals uses explicit profile/listing word overlap and makes no hiring probability or improvement claim.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Required language discipline:** Never call the displayed percentage a probability of interview, offer, employment, or success.

#### US 10,380,552 B2: applicant skill inference

Source: [US10380552B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10380552B2/en)

The claim uses job and member databases, application indicators, an applicant threshold, and profiles of applicants to infer skills for a job. fear.social does not maintain an applicant corpus, application threshold, or inferred skill model.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Re-review trigger:** Importing applicants, applications, resumes, or inferring skills from who applied.

#### US 11,461,421 B2: skill suggestions

Source: [US11461421B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11461421B2/en)

fear.social does not recommend skills to add to a profile.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.

#### Additional LinkedIn families screened

- [US8972417B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8972417B2/en), communication-recipient suggestions: DMs are user selected and not recommended from drafted text.
- [US8856250B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8856250B2/en), community notifications based on profile updates: profile edits do not trigger community broadcasts.
- [US9654592B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9654592B2/en), skills endorsements: fear.social has no endorsement system.

### ByteDance and TikTok

#### US 12,159,298: predictive recommendation system

Source: [US12159298](https://patents.justia.com/patent/12159298)

The claims use engagement data with time intervals and activation behavior to predict a consumer state, generate a ranking, and transmit a top-N result. fear.social does not collect dwell intervals or activation behavior, predict a consumer state, or use that state to select top-N media.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Re-review trigger:** Watch time, replay rate, pause behavior, app reactivation, inferred intent, session-state prediction, or top-N learned media selection.

#### US 11,936,954 B2: processing video recommendations

Source: [US11936954B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11936954)

The reviewed claim requires video-recommendation information identifying recommending users and comments, a recommendation control in a comment or forwarding interface, and recommendation of the video to an associated third user. fear.social has like, comment, and save controls but no “recommend this video to a friend” control or recommendation metadata in comments.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.

#### US 12,314,528 B2: displaying a feed flow

Source: [US12314528B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12314528B2/en)

The claim is directed to a particular swipe-feed and interactive-content process. fear.social uses ordinary multi-card document scrolling and has no one-item swipe playback flow.

**Preliminary risk:** Low.  
**Re-review trigger:** Full-screen vertical media pagination, auto-advance, or interactive material inserted into that playback flow.

#### TikTok graphical-interface design patents

- [USD788137S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD788137S1/en)
- [USD1008282S1](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD1008282S1/en)

Design patents protect ornamental appearance rather than functionality. fear.social’s current card-based black, white, and green professional interface does not present the same overall arrangement as the reviewed TikTok designs. A native-app launch still warrants visual design clearance against the complete D14 design class and all relevant drawings. The [USPTO design patent guide](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/design-patent) explains that the drawings define the protected appearance.

**Preliminary risk:** Low-to-moderate because final design comparison is visual and fact-specific.  
**Safeguards:** Preserve distinct typography, color, navigation, icon treatment, card composition, and ordinary page scrolling. Do not recreate TikTok’s exact full-screen player controls or Instagram’s Stories/Reels composition.

### VaynerMedia and Complex Media

Exact-name assignee and publication searches did not identify a U.S. social-networking patent family that maps directly to the implemented features. That is **not proof that no relevant rights exist**. Patents may be assigned to parent companies, acquisition vehicles, founders, inventors, licensors, or differently named subsidiaries. These companies also raise separate trademark, copyright, content-licensing, publicity-right, and trade-dress questions that this patent review does not resolve.

**Preliminary patent risk from exact-name results:** No specific claim identified.  
**Counsel follow-up:** Search USPTO Assignment Center, subsidiaries, acquisitions, inventor names, and licensed technology.

## Non-patent overlap discovered during review

“Reels” is strongly associated with Meta/Instagram branding even though this report does not determine trademark rights. The product has therefore renamed official short-form posts **Daily Drops**. The personalized feed label is **Your Path**, and the non-personalized people list is **New people**. These changes improve product accuracy and reduce avoidable brand and trade-dress comparison.

## Product changes made in this review

- Restored ordinary document scrolling across browsers and retained card-based page flow instead of a patented-style swipe player.
- Renamed the personalized feed to **Your Path**.
- Renamed **Suggested people** to **New people** because the list is not generated by profile similarity.
- Renamed official **Reels** to **Daily Drops**, including a database migration and backwards-compatible rendering.
- Added architecture guardrails in `PATENT_SAFE_PRODUCT_RULES.md`.

## Attorney handoff checklist

Before treating this as a formal freedom-to-operate opinion, counsel should:

1. Confirm the target countries, launch date, corporate entity, and exact production behavior.
2. Run professional searches in USPTO Patent Public Search, Patent Center, Google Patents, Lens, and relevant international databases.
3. Search CPC/USPC classes and forward/backward citations, not only assignee names.
4. Expand assignee searches to subsidiaries, acquisitions, inventors, licensors, and assignment records.
5. Verify live legal status, maintenance fees, patent-term adjustment, terminal disclaimers, certificates, reexaminations, PTAB history, and pending continuations.
6. Prepare element-by-element claim charts for every still-material independent claim and any dependent claim covering an implemented variation.
7. Review prosecution history and proposed claim constructions.
8. Conduct a separate design-patent comparison using current screenshots at desktop and mobile sizes.
9. Conduct separate trademark/trade-dress clearance for product names, labels, icons, and marketing language.
10. Re-run the analysis before launching ML ranking, Stories, full-screen vertical video, skill inference, applicant scoring, recipient suggestions, or ranked comments/notifications.

## Bottom line

The present implementation has sensible technical separation from the specific patent claims reviewed, but no engineering review can make the product “impossible to sue” or replace a signed legal opinion. The safest business path is to preserve these separations, keep a dated feature-decision record, and give this report plus the production code and screenshots to qualified U.S. patent counsel for formal clearance.
